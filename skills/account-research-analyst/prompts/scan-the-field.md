# Scan the Field — Who Is Circling This Problem Space

<instructions>
Map who else is circling this target and segment, what frame they use, and what
language the buyer has already been sold — so scoring, storyline, and offering-fit
know the competitive water the account is swimming in. Output is a section in the
account profile (or `v{n}.md`). **Gate rule:** every peer artefact is about *their*
client — usable as a market pattern, **never** as a fact about this target.
</instructions>

<process>
1. Scan across **classes of entity**, not a fixed name list, and record why you skipped
   any class. The generic classes: broad consultancies; tech-services players moving
   up-stack with agentic AI; hyperscalers; foundation-model providers going vertical;
   LLM-native vertical AI; established category platforms; specialist point tools;
   geographic equivalents; adjacent-industry equivalents when the target is off-ICP.
   For the sector-specific roster, read the matching `docs/overlays/` file.

2. Collect three things, each cited:
   - Which **use cases** are already landing in the segment.
   - **Who is circling** this target/segment, and with what **frame**.
   - What **language** the buyer has already been sold (this shapes how we must
     differentiate, and what vocabulary is already burned).

3. **Run the negative:** search for a published customer story that **names this
   target**. A clean "no" is a "be the first named proof" offer — record it as such.
   A "yes" tells you who is already inside.

4. Note the target's own external auditor / assurance provider if relevant — that firm
   will pitch them adjacent tooling too (a class of competitor, not a fact about the
   target's buying).

5. Apply the **gate rule** to everything: peer case studies and analyst mentions are
   *market pattern* (**Assume**, `[confirm]` if projected onto this target), never a
   **Know** about this account. Do not attribute any reference customer's detail to the
   target.

6. Write the section: the field by class, who is circling and their frame, the
   already-sold language, and the negative-search result.
</process>
