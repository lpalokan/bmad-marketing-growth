# Source fidelity in a dossier

Extends the standard Source Fidelity block in every SKILL.md. Where the two
differ, the stricter rule wins.

## Three labels, applied at capture

Every load-bearing fact carries one label, written once, at the moment it enters
the record. Downstream agents inherit the label and do not re-derive it.

- **KNOW.** Verified this session against a named source, with a working link.
  `(KNOW: [FY2025 results release](url))`
- **ASSUME.** Our own reasoning, inference or read. Always visibly labelled.
  `(ASSUME on relevance to this account.)`
- **GAP.** Something we do not know, named precisely. Every GAP becomes a
  numbered discovery question and appears in the sources block.

An unlabelled fact is not publishable. If you cannot label it, you do not know it.

## Never invent

Never invent a number, a customer name, a person, a title, a metric, a benchmark
or a case study. This includes plausible-sounding defaults and industry averages.
If a step needs a figure nobody has given us, it is a GAP.

## Verify live, in session

These decay and must be checked in the session that publishes, never recalled
from memory or inherited without a date:

- People. Names, exact titles, whether they are still in seat.
- Penalties, enforcement actions, supervisory processes and their dates.
- Prices, commercial terms and published offers.
- Anything the dossier will use to justify contacting a specific person.

A leader or penalty carried from memory is an automatic failure at the
pre-publish gate.

## Show discrepancies, do not resolve them silently

When two sources disagree, put both in the dossier and flag the conflict.

> The 2025 financial statements name 14 savings banks. The website says 15.
> `[CONFIRM]` which is current, most likely a merger between the two publication
> dates.

Silently picking one is how a dossier acquires a fact nobody can trace. Showing
the conflict also hands the AE a legitimate opening question.

## Our own proof points are claims until proven otherwise

Proof points from our offering pages are **stated claims from our own site**.
They are not independently verified metrics and they are not tied to a named
reference customer unless one exists.

- Present them as claims, with the source link.
- Where a named reference would carry the argument and we do not have one, write
  `[PROOF NEEDED: what]` and leave it visible.
- Never invent a comparable, a logo or a metric to fill the hole.
- When a buyer asks who else uses this, the dossier's answer is to take the
  question away and come back with what the offering team can actually cite.

## Absence of evidence

Record it explicitly and label it. "No enforcement action against this group was
found" is a useful, honest finding. Write it as a KNOW-absence with the date and
the search basis, and never let it drift into "they have a clean record".

## Quoting

- Quote the source's own words for anything load-bearing, in the original
  language where it matters, with a plain translation alongside.
- Keep quotes short and never stitch two into one.
- Where a public body's exact wording carries the argument, quote it verbatim.

## The self-audit question

Before publishing any fact that came from a research pass, ask: did I fetch this,
or do I recall it. If the answer is recall, it is not KNOW. This question has
caught real fabrications, including agents presenting search summaries as
verified sources.
