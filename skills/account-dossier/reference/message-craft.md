# First-touch messages

The messages section is the part of the dossier an AE will actually paste and
edit, so it has to be usable as written and safe to send after light editing.

## Scope

One message per load-bearing seat, **three at most** (`length-budget.md`). Where
more seats matter, write to the ones that carry the opening and name the rest in
section 3 as parallel threads. Add:

- A note the champion can forward internally, written in their voice about us.
- A LinkedIn connection note for the lead buyer.
- A pre-send checklist.

Do not write a message for every name in the committee. A seat gets a message
when we have something specific to say to that person.

## The standing label

Every messages section opens with this, verbatim:

> *Examples, illustrative. Every claim and figure must be re-checked against the
> source before sending. Nothing has been sent. Each message is a recommendation
> for a human to review, edit and send.*

Nothing in a dossier is ever sent by the motion that wrote it.

## Anatomy

- **Subject.** Six words or fewer. About their situation, never about us.
- **Opening line.** Their why-now, in their own terms. No throat-clearing, no
  compliments, no "I hope this finds you well".
- **The turn.** One sentence naming the thing that is hard about their situation
  and easy to underestimate. This is the pillar tailored to their seat.
- **What we do.** Two or three sentences. Plain description, no adjectives.
- **The ask.** One, small, specific. A working session beats a meeting. A dated
  reason to talk now beats an open invitation.

Around 120 words. One idea per message. No brochure dumps.

## Rules

- **Tailor to the seat, from the storyline.** Each message advances the pillar
  the committee map assigns to that person. Never send the same argument twice
  with the name changed.
- **No third parties.** See `third-parties.md`. This is absolute.
- **No headcount reduction language.** Ever.
- **No implication that the account failed.** Where a public finding exists,
  reference the standard it implies rather than the finding, and only with the
  seats that own it.
- **Do not assert an outcome that has not happened.** Where a transaction or
  approval is pending, write conditionally. "If the offer completes" is accurate
  and sounds better informed than assuming.
- **Every figure in a message must exist in the dossier**, with its source.
- **No personal contact detail** in the dossier body.
- Follow `house-style.md` for sentence rules. No em dashes, no "X, not Y".

## Where a buyer has unusual authority

When a buyer has a background that makes them a harder audience, such as a former
regulator now inside the risk function, write to that rather than around it. Ask
them to test the thing they are qualified to test. A question they are uniquely
able to answer outperforms a pitch they are uniquely able to dismantle.

## The pre-send checklist

Every dossier ends its messages section with a checklist tailored to the account.
It always includes:

- [ ] Re-read the most recent results release and confirm the figures used.
- [ ] Confirm any pending event has not changed status, with its date.
- [ ] Re-verify every enrichment-derived name and title.
- [ ] Remove any reference to a third party.
- [ ] Confirm any regulatory matter is described in the right terms.
- [ ] Set a real send date. Nothing here is scheduled.

Add account-specific items: seasonal timing, a results date, a live offer period,
a language preference.
